Where the data comes from

The science numbers in MarsCraft (radiation, sunlight, air, distance, crew needs) come from open NASA data. Speeds, sizes and costs are game rules, and each screen says which is which. Most data is saved inside the game with its date, so it is not live. Only space weather tries to load LIVE from NASA DONKI when the game opens. Without internet, the game uses the saved data and never says LIVE.

Solar stormsReal CMEs (clouds of particles thrown by the Sun) that NASA predicted would reach Mars, listed by NASA DONKI. The warning time is the trip NASA predicted, squeezed so 1 real day is 1 game hour. .
Space weather liveWhen the game opens it asks NASA DONKI for the CMEs of the last 30 days, the bursts of super fast particles (SEP) of the last 60 days and the newest NASA space weather bulletin, shown on the radio at the science station. If NASA sends no SEP, the game does not make one up. If the internet fails or no CME is headed to Mars, the game uses the data saved inside it.
Storm strengthA game rule: a faster CME makes a stronger storm. It always stays between two real storms measured by the Curiosity rover: the one in 2017 that doubled the radiation for two days, and the biggest ever, in 2024 (8,100 uGy, like 30 chest X-rays). How much it hurts is also a game rule.
RadiationCuriosity rover RAD instrument at Gale Crater, from NASA RadLab (OSDR): 6.93 uGy per hour, measured 16/11 to 10/12/2022.
Trip doseOn the way to Mars, Curiosity's RAD measured 1.8 mSv per day inside the spacecraft (Zeitlin et al., Science, 2013). A 203-day trip like Mars 2020 adds about 365 mSv before landing. On Mars, the game counts each game hour like about 8 days (a game rule).
GroundThe shape of the land is real. It is a small crater inside Gale Crater on Mars (), measured from orbit by HiRISE, a camera on NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter. One block is about 1 meter. Real data from . The flat ground under the base and the ice pit were shaped for the game.
Mars factsGravity 38% of Earth, a day of 24 h 39 min and 43% of Earth sunlight, from the NASA Mars Fact Sheet.
WeatherReal data from the NASA InSight lander at Elysium Planitia, sols 675 to 681 (October 2020): low and high temperature, air pressure and wind gusts. Each game sol uses one real sol, in order. The heater uses more power when the real air is colder. The shape of the temperature curve during the day is a game rule.
Dust stormsDust in the air is measured as "tau" (how much sunlight it blocks). A clear sky at the Opportunity rover was about 0.5; the 2018 global storm reached 10.8, the highest ever measured on the ground (NASA JPL). Mars air is too thin to push you over: the danger is the dust, which hides the Sun and covers solar panels. When storms come, how long they last and how much power they cut are game rules.
Mars eventsReal events: the InSight lander felt more than 1,300 marsquakes (the biggest, magnitude 5, on 04/05/2022) and a meteor impact on 24/12/2021, whose new crater with ice was found by the MRO orbiter. Perseverance filmed Phobos crossing the Sun (02/04/2022) and detected a green aurora (March 2024). Spirit got its solar panels cleaned by the wind in March 2005, maybe by a dust devil. Viking 2 photographed frost on the ground (1977). When events happen and how strong they are is a game rule, picked by the mission seed.
Crew needsOxygen, water and food per person from the NASA Baseline Values and Assumptions Document. Water recycling of 98% like the Space Station.
Making airTwo real machines, and you pick one. The Space Station Oxygen Generation Assembly (OGA) splits water: 36 g of water make 32 g of oxygen, so one person needs about 0.95 L of water a day for air (NASA, 2015). A Sabatier reactor turns leftover hydrogen and the CO2 we breathe out back into water, but gets back only about half (NASA ICES, 2024). MOXIE on the Perseverance rover pulled 122 g of oxygen out of Mars air, at best 12 g per hour (NASA, 2023). How much power each machine uses is a game rule.
Space biologyReal studies about bones, muscles, radiation, rest and the immune system from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), and the radiation comparison from NASA RadLab. They unlock in your journal [J], which shows the date the data was saved.
HealthCareer radiation limit of 600 mSv (NASA-STD-3001). Bones lose about 1% of density a month in space without exercise (NASA, 2023).
SleepAstronauts on the Space Shuttle and the Space Station slept about 6 hours a night, with 8.5 hours planned (study of 85 astronauts, Barger et al., 2014). How much food and water you use while you sleep is a game rule.
Night powerThe fission reactor in the Power & Heat room is based on Kilopower, a small reactor NASA tested in 2018 (1 to 10 kW, day and night). Its power and the radiation it adds are game rules: real reactors stay far from the crew or behind a heavy shield.

Game speed, battery size and storm length are scaled so a mission fits in a few minutes. The start menu space art was generated with AI assistance. Other art, textures and sounds are drawn by code, with no NASA logo. Parts of the code were written with AI assistance.

MAV - Mars Ascent Vehicle

0% ready

This rocket takes you back to orbit, on your way home. Finish all three jobs, then press IGNITE.

1. Rocket fuel: 0%

The fuel factory splits Martian ice into hydrogen and oxygen, then mixes the hydrogen with carbon dioxide from the Mars air to make methane. Methane + oxygen = rocket fuel. It needs ice and uses energy.

2. Solar storms survived: 0/3

A solar storm starts when the Sun throws out a huge cloud of fast particles (scientists call it a CME). Stay alive until 3 storms pass: hide in the base or turn on the shield with the Shield Remote (hotbar slot 3).

3. Science sent to Earth: No

Scan things with the scope and analyze regolith samples, then send them from the science & radio terminal inside the base (Send button, or [G] next to it). A bigger package uses more energy.

Extra: rock sample tubes on board: 0

Real rovers like Perseverance seal rock samples in tubes so a rocket can one day bring them to Earth. A heavier rocket needs more fuel, so each tube asks for more ice.
